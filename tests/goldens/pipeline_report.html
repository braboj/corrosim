Of the 3 molecules screened, quercetin ranks highest on the composite electronic score (+1.34), combining the smallest HOMO–LUMO gap (3.68 eV) with the highest softness — the most readily polarised, electron-donating candidate of the set. It adsorbs flat on Fe in the physisorption regime (E_ads ≈ -16 kJ/mol). The lead holds across all 4 descriptor bases evaluated (geometry and protonation). This is a computational screening prediction requiring electrochemical confirmation (see the Method section and caveats).

6. Method & caveats

DFT level: B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) (ddCOSMO:water). Global descriptors come from the frontier-orbital energies via Koopmans' theorem; ΔN uses the metal work function with η(metal) = 0. The Monte-Carlo and Brownian-MD stages use a classical van-der-Waals adsorption model — a physics-based screening surrogate, not a substitute for periodic DFT or for electrochemical validation. The composite ranking is a heuristic that orders candidates; it does not prove inhibition. Full methodology: docs/pipeline.md; validation record: docs/validation.md.
